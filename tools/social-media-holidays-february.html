Social Media Holidays in February 2026

February squeezes Super Bowl Sunday on February 8, Valentine's Day on February 14 and Lunar New Year on February 17 into a single three week run. Here is the whole month with dates, hashtags and the ones worth clearing your calendar for.

Last updated: 2026-08-03

February is short and unusually dense. Super Bowl Sunday on February 8 pulls an enormous live audience for one evening, Valentine's Day on February 14 is the second biggest gifting moment of the year for anything you can wrap, and Lunar New Year on February 17 opens a fifteen day celebration that matters to a large part of the world and gets ignored by most Western brands. Three genuinely large moments inside ten days is more concentration than any other month on this calendar has to offer.

It is also the month where the legal and tonal traps are thickest. The Super Bowl name is a registered trademark and unlicensed commercial use of it is a real risk, which is why so many brands say the big game instead. Valentine's Day is a painful day for a chunk of any mailing list, so the accounts that handle it best offer an obvious way out. And Lunar New Year is a specific cultural celebration with its own zodiac year and its own customs, not a generic red and gold background. Galentine's Day on February 13 sits there for anyone whose audience is not shopping for a partner, and Rare Disease Day closes the month on February 28.

The table below lists all 14 February observances in this calendar, 3 of them flagged as top picks, with the hashtag next to each one so you are not guessing at the spelling. 3 of them move from year to year and are resolved here for 2026, each with a note in the table saying which rule it follows. Nothing on this page is gated: the email at the bottom is the year at a glance in one message, for people who would rather plan twelve months in one sitting than come back to twelve pages.

How to use the February list

A calendar like this is a menu, not a schedule. Three passes is usually all it takes to turn the table above into a month you can actually publish.

Cut it down first

Read the whole month once and keep only the dates a customer would connect to what you sell. Two or three is the right number. The top picks are the widest-recognition dates, so they are the natural shortlist, but a smaller day that fits your niche exactly beats a big one that does not.

Work backwards from the date

Anything that needs photography, a landing page or stock on a shelf needs two to three weeks of lead time, so a date in the last week of February is really a briefing in the first. Put the deadline in your calendar, not the observance.

Fill the gaps, do not lead with them

Your customer stories, product news and the things you know better than anyone else come first. Observances are for the days between them, which is exactly where most feeds go quiet.

Posting around February without forcing it

  • Say "the big game" unless you have paid for the rights. Super Bowl is a registered trademark, and using it to promote a product is the kind of thing that generates a letter. The audience knows exactly what you mean either way.
  • Set your Valentine's Day shipping cutoff and publish it. Anything physical needs to be ordered days ahead of February 14, and the post that saves the sale is the one that says so, not the one that says buy now on February 13.
  • Offer a Valentine's opt-out on your email list. A single line letting people skip the campaign costs you nothing and keeps the subscribers who would otherwise unsubscribe in the second week of February.
  • Galentine's Day on February 13 carries the accounts that Valentine's Day does not fit. Friendship, teams and communities are an easier story than romance for most brands, and the day before is far less crowded than the day itself.
  • Research before you post for Lunar New Year on February 17. It is a specific celebration with a zodiac animal and real customs attached, and a post that gets the year wrong is more visible than no post at all.

Frequently asked questions

Are these dates the same every year?

Some are, some are not. Fixed dates such as Valentine's Day on February 14 are the same every year and can be planned indefinitely ahead. 3 February entries float: Super Bowl Sunday on February 8, Lunar New Year on February 17 and Rare Disease Day on February 28 this year. Every floating entry is resolved for 2026 and carries a note in the table explaining the rule behind it, which tells you exactly which rows to re-check when the year rolls over.

Do I have to post for every one of these?

Definitely not, and trying is the fastest way to make a feed look automated. 14 observances in February is a menu, not a quota. Pick the two or three a customer would actually connect to what you sell, and let the rest go by without comment. The table flags 3 top picks for February, Super Bowl Sunday on February 8, Valentine's Day on February 14 and Lunar New Year on February 17, as the ones with the widest recognition, and even those only earn a post if you have something real to say on the day.

When is Lunar New Year in 2026, and can I just reuse last year's post?

Lunar New Year falls on February 17 in 2026 and it moves every year, because it follows a lunisolar calendar rather than the Gregorian one. Reusing an old post is also a bad idea for a second reason: each year carries a different zodiac animal, so last year's artwork is visibly wrong.
